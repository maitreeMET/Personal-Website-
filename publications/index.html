    Racing the Clock: Teaching VLNs Budget-Conditioned Object Search
    Maitree Meher, Siming He, S. Shankar Sastry, Jitendra Malik, Claire Tomlin, and Jonas Frey
    In IROS 2026 Workshop on Intelligent Information Gathering, 2026
    Best Paper Award Abs Bib PDF

    Best Paper Award at the IROS 2026 Workshop on Intelligent Information Gathering.

    How do action budgets shape vision-language navigation (VLN) exploration, and how does the source of supervision affect learned search? We study budget-conditioned object search: finding as many distinct instances of a requested category as possible within a budget, with discovery defined geometrically by camera field of view and proximity. Distinct discoveries provide a denser evaluation signal than single-target success. We compare two supervision schemes: an object-level Bellman expert routes among targets with the full map and locations, while a room-level Bellman expert represents unexplored rooms through semantic estimates and cross-scene priors, leaving their unseen contents unknown. Both optimize budgeted Bellman objectives within their scene abstractions. We distill them into Uni-NaVid under matched training conditions and also evaluate greedy-frontier supervision on held-out HM3D scenes. Changing only the stated budget changes learned trajectories. Despite higher object-level expert return, room-level supervision achieves the highest mean collection among evaluated VLNs at every tested budget. At 2000 steps, it finds 72.7% of reachable targets, versus 68.5% under greedy-frontier supervision, 65.5% under object-level supervision, and 44.8% for the pretrained VLN. These results show that expert-return rankings need not predict supervision quality for VLN exploration and motivate accounting for budget and expert search behavior when collecting demonstrations.
